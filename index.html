    About

    How to build here — then a few chemistry ideas the 3D stage makes real.

    How to use

    • Library — pick a category chip or search, tap a molecule, watch it assemble in 3D. Type a letter to jump to best name matches (a → Acetaminophen, Adenine…).
    • Element rail — swipe the table, tap an element to place it. First atom sits at the center. Each tile shows Z, symbol, name, and room-temp state (gas / liquid / solid / ?).
    • Open sites — glowing ghosts show where the next atom can bond. Tap a ghost or tap the element again to auto-place.
    • Spin & zoom — drag to tumble freely; pinch to zoom.
    • Undo — in free-build, tap the last chip in the ingredient tray to remove the last atom. Library molecules keep the tray locked (Clear or start building to edit).
    • Clear — wipe the stage and start fresh.

    Chemistry bites

    • Atoms & bonds — atoms are the balls; bonds are the sticks. A molecule is a connected pattern of both.
    • Valence — how many bonds an element “wants.” Oxygen usually wants 2, carbon 4, hydrogen 1. Gray tiles mean that element can’t attach right now.
    • Shapes — water bends (~104.5°), methane is tetrahedral, boron trifluoride is flat. The free-build tries to follow those textbook angles.
    • Covalent vs ionic — shared electrons (water, caffeine) vs charged partners (table salt). The library tags both.
    • Names & stories — free-build keeps a live name when it can; library cards add discovery year, place, and a short hook to teach and inspire.
    • States on the rail — gas, liquid, and solid are room-temperature teaching labels; ? means we don’t have a settled everyday state (superheavy odds and ends).

    Try this

    • Build water: O, then H, then H — watch the bent shape.
    • Open Library → Everyday & kitchen and assemble caffeine, sucrose, or citric acid.
    • Open Library → Body & medicine and assemble adrenaline or the DNA helix.
    • In Everyday, play Structured water — a hydrogen-bonded hexagon labeled (H₂O)₆, not H₃O₂.
    • Swipe the rail past Oganesson to find mythical Unobtainium (Uo, Z 119) — an element, not a library molecule.
    • Spin the art pieces: cubane, C₆₀, porphyrin, cholesterol, or an 11-base-pair DNA helix snippet.
    • Free-build nonsense like HB₁₁ — the app allows it; chemistry may not. That’s the lesson.
